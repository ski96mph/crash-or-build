Beauty and the Menace

This is really more like 2 MOCs presented together but I don't want to make separate posts. So the flower came about when I obtained the at the time recently released leaf piece in bulk in dark azure and aqua. I was certain someone was going to make a big flower with them and I wanted to be the first. Of course, I didn't end up posting it for over a year but I had some photos with timestamps in the metadata and Discord messages to prove my superior speed and intellect, yet, to my amazement, no one else ever ended up building anything similar, to my knowledge. It does quite well at conventions so definitely a success, especially for its simplicity.

There's not a lot to say about the build process for the flower, it's just a bunch of leaves on clips connected to octagonal clip frames, large steering wheels, and big rings. There was a bit of trial and error getting the spacing, the vertical step downs, and the spreading right but I don't have any deep insights to give you here. It was just tedious. And the geometry very much does not work out, by the time the whole flower is put together there are a bunch of petals that have been disconnected internally and are being held in place by sheer force of will (by which I mean being jammed between all the other petals that are barely hanging on themselves and have nowhere to go).

I then decided I wanted to spice up the presentation a bit with a creature to go with it, and what better than to make an insect of some sort to make it a spiritual successor of one of my earliest "successful" MOCs from 2009? Hey man, I was in middle school and Lego was very different back then. Selecting the insect was somewhat arbitrary, I just wanted something recognizable and not a fly, since I did that for the '09 MOC, and not a mosquito, because VB's from a year later still lives rent free in my mind.

Two factors led to the wasp: 1) I knew yellow would be a nice contrast against the azure flower and 2) I ended up intrigued by the challenge of building the striping of a wasp abdomen without stickers.

The shoulder shells seemed obvious for the top of the abdomen, similar had been done many times before. And the spoilers on the bottom also seemed an obvious solution for the concave surface. But how to do the sides? By convenient happenstance, the smallest technic panels are of the right size and right angle to make an appropriate curve. I then needed to figure out how to connect them when both of their standard connection points would interfere with other parts. My first instinct was to try stringing them on a whip or string, but that didn't work out. Eventually experimentation led to this beautiful abomination with bucket handles and skeleton arms. It's more stable than I would expect once it's all folded up but less than I'd like.

It does open up nicely, I'll say that for it.

The scale at this stage also made it apparent that I would likely be using Gorast wings for wings. Not the best shape, but good size and ok color, so eh, good enough.

Once the abdomen was done I focused on the head, the next most important and recognizable part of the insect. I wouldn't say anything super interesting is going on here but I did feel pretty clever using the stud notches of the baby wedges to form the serrated edges of the mandibles. I learned a lot about wasp anatomy using reference images, their funky eye shape and hanging palpi caught me by surprise. My attempt at capturing the eye shape is a little weak, and I also wish I could give them a different texture to stand out more against all the other black in the build, but I did my best.

The thorax went through two complete structural redesigns after the initial build as a round on the convention circuit convinced me the connections for the middle set of legs were not strong enough. The final solution is the most illegal of the three designs (sorry, not comparison shots) but the joints are strong enough to support the full weight of the build more consistently than the previous designs and it's slightly less frustrating to rebuild. But again, there's not much of particular interest here, either, it's mostly just a brick of system SNOT put together with a fair bit of trial and error as I tried to figure out how the middle set of legs could be held together.

The legs are obviously quite simple, but I thought the pinched texture on the flex tube was a decent solution at this scale. Both rear sets of legs had fixed right angle technic connectors for strength so it could support its own weight.

For a long time I was just displaying the flower and wasp sitting next to each other at conventions and at home, so my final challenge was getting the wasp to actually sit on the flower. The flower petals are all pretty fragilely attached so it wasn't trivial to make the stand that would blend in ok, but I think I managed pretty well at the cost of only a couple petals. With the stand bearing the weight of the wasp, the strength of the right angle conenctors was, of course, superfluous, so I got rid of them on the hind legs for a tighter, more natural angle. The middle legs weren't in such desperate need of the more acute angle so I left than as is out of laziness (also the espresso plate joint construction does affect the length of the segments and I didn't feel like messing with that).

I did consider a more plant-like base than rectangular black block, but I was feeling pretty done with the build at that point and it wouldn't have been trivial to accomplish. So you get a black rectangular block.

Besides the awkward eye shape, other disappointments include the pretty lackluster attempt at the secondary wings and the too-narrow width of the abdomen, it should really be much fatter. And a little bigger in general. But this construction was the basis of the whole build so I wasn't interested in going back to change it. Besides which, this MOC falls under the "casual build" category in my mind. This was no Sagittarius.

This also marked my first experimentation with a turntable for 360 video. Hopefully you appreciate the extra work I had to do for it. While I prefer static images for examining a MOC, I do think video does a lot to convey sense of presence.

The flower probably deserves its own photoshoot since it's so very aesthetic, but it's definitely not happening anytime soon.
